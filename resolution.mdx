Every market on Outcome ends with an answer. Here's how we get to it.

Outcome runs its own resolution system, built with established oracle and resolution partners. And you don't have to take our word for it: every settled Price Binary and Price Touch market gets a public proof on Arbitrum One, recorded by a Chainlink network of 10 nodes. The nodes recalculate Price Binary results and Price Touch Yes results from price data. A Price Touch No is recorded as Outcome's attestation for now. See [Settlement proofs](/settlement-proofs).

For real-world events, the goal during mainnet rollout is to resolve within 4 hours of the official result from authoritative sources, and to bring that down to seconds over time.

## Price markets

All price markets settle on one source: the HyperCore perpetual **<u>mark price</u>** for the market's underlying. Instead of a single price print, they use a **TWAP** (time-weighted average price), so one stray trade can't swing the result. Want the math? [TWAP calculation](/twap-calculation) walks through it with real numbers.

### Price Binary

Was the price above the strike at expiry? Outcome averages the price over the last X seconds **before** expiry and compares that TWAP to the strike. Nothing after expiry is used. X is set per contract and scales with the contract period. For the 15 minute Up/Down markets, it's the 60 seconds right before expiry.

- TWAP **strictly above** the strike: settles **Yes**.
- TWAP **at or below** the strike: settles **No**. Landing exactly on the strike is a No.

### Price Touch

Did the price ever reach the strike while the market was open? Outcome watches a rolling 1 second TWAP of the underlying rather than raw prints. A touch registers when that TWAP trades at or through the strike. Once it does, the contract settles Yes right away and stays Yes, wherever the price goes next. If the TWAP never gets there before expiry, it settles No.

Because the touch uses a TWAP, a quick wick that reverts within the same second won't necessarily count. What matters is whether the average across that second clears the strike, not whether any single print did.

### Price Scalar

Instead of a straight Yes or No, a scalar market settles to a value inside a range. Each contract has a lower and an upper bound, and Outcome uses the same TWAP over the seconds right before expiry:

- At or below the lower bound: settles at 0.
- At or above the upper bound: settles at 1.
- Anywhere in between: settles proportionally along the range.

<Note>
Resolution is currently in Beta. Markets settle with a short buffer while we check the data against the official sources listed in the rules. Once Outcome exits Beta, deployment and settlement will be fully automated, so markets deploy and resolve near instantly.
</Note>
