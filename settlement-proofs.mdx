When an Outcome price market settles, you shouldn't have to take our word for the result. A network of Chainlink computers checks it against the market's rules and records a proof on Arbitrum One, a public blockchain. Anyone can look it up, and nobody can change it afterwards.

Settlement proofs cover the two price market types Outcome deploys: **Price Binary** (including Up/Down markets) and **Price Touch** (Hit Price markets).

## What Chainlink CRE is

The [Chainlink Runtime Environment (CRE)](https://docs.chain.link/cre/key-terms#chainlink-runtime-environment-cre) runs small programs, called [workflows](https://docs.chain.link/cre/key-terms#workflow), on a [Decentralized Oracle Network (DON)](https://docs.chain.link/cre/key-terms#decentralized-oracle-network-don): a group of computers called **nodes**. Every node runs the same program and gets its own copy of the data, and the network only accepts an answer that enough nodes agree on.

Outcome's workflow runs on 10 nodes. A result needs at least 4 of them to come back with exactly the same answer, so one node with bad data or a bug can't produce a proof on its own.

## How a proof is made

Settlement and the proof run side by side. Your market settles on Hyperliquid as usual (see [Resolution and settlement](/resolution-and-settlement)), and the proof never delays or changes your payout.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The market settles">
    Outcome settles the market on Hyperliquid and asks the CRE workflow to check it. For a Price Binary market, Outcome sends only the market's ID: no price and no result. For a Price Touch market, it also sends what it found: when the touch happened, or that there was none.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Each node looks up the market and the prices">
    Each node reads the market's rules (strike, expiry, TWAP window) from the market's own record and confirms it's a genuine Outcome market. Then it fetches the recorded HyperCore mark prices from Hydromancer, a Hyperliquid data service.

    All nodes use that same price source. So the proof shows the result was **calculated correctly from the recorded prices**. It doesn't show that several independent sources agreed on the price.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Each node works out the result">
    For **Price Binary**, the node calculates the TWAP over the seconds before expiry and compares it to the strike. For a **Price Touch Yes**, it recalculates the rolling TWAP around the reported touch and checks that it reached the target. A **Price Touch No** isn't recalculated yet: see [What gets checked](#what-gets-checked).

    [TWAP calculation](/twap-calculation) walks through the math with an example.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The nodes compare answers">
    Each node's answer is a complete record: the result, the settlement price, the time window, and fingerprints of the rules and price data it used. The network accepts it only when at least 4 nodes return **exactly** the same record, every field included, and no other record gets 4.

    If the prices aren't available yet or the nodes don't match, nothing is recorded and the check runs again later. A missing proof means the check hasn't finished, not that the result was wrong. Your settlement isn't affected either way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The proof is recorded">
    The network signs the agreed record, and Chainlink saves it to Outcome's proof contract on Arbitrum One. The contract only accepts records from Outcome's approved workflow, and only one per market. A proof usually appears a few minutes after the market settles.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What gets checked

| Market and result | What the nodes check |
| --- | --- |
| Price Binary, either side | Recalculate the TWAP from the recorded prices and compare it to the strike |
| Price Touch, Yes | Recalculate the rolling TWAP around the reported touch and confirm it reached the target |
| Price Touch, No | Confirm the market is genuine and the deadline has passed. The prices aren't rechecked: the proof records Outcome's statement that no touch happened |

Checking that a price **never** reached a target means reading every price over a market's whole life, which can be weeks. That doesn't fit in one CRE run today, so for now a Price Touch No is Outcome's word, recorded publicly. We're working on having the nodes check it from the prices too.

## See a proof

On a settled Price Binary or Price Touch market on [outcome.xyz](https://outcome.xyz), open **Settlement Proof** under the market's rules. On mobile, it's a row below the rules.

The popup has two tabs:

- **Calculations** shows the result, the settlement rule, the TWAP window, and the exact TWAP or touch time.
- **Proof details** shows the proof's identifiers and fingerprints, with a link to the **Settlement transaction** on [Arbiscan](https://arbiscan.io), Arbitrum's public block explorer. Follow it to see the proof on the blockchain itself, without relying on Outcome.

## What's in a proof

| Field | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | The side the market settled on: Yes or No, shown with the market's own labels (for example Up or Down). |
| Market type | Price Binary or Price Touch. |
| Settlement price | For Price Binary, the exact TWAP that was compared to the strike. For Price Touch, the target price. A Yes means the TWAP reached it: on "Will HYPE hit $80 by the end of November?", a 3 second TWAP at or above 80.000 settles Yes, and a target that's never reached settles No. |
| Settlement time | When the price used for the result was observed. For a Price Touch No, the deadline. |
| Rules fingerprint | A hash of the market's rules, so they can't be swapped after the fact. |
| Evidence fingerprint | A hash of the exact market record and prices the nodes used, so anyone with the same data can reproduce the result. |
| Workflow | The CRE workflow that produced the proof. |

## Which markets have proofs

- **Price Binary** and **Price Touch** markets that Outcome deploys.
- Not **Price Range** markets: Hyperliquid deploys and settles those, so they have no Outcome proof.
- Not yet **real-world event** markets, such as sports or economic data. See [Real-world events](/real-world-events) for how those resolve.
